Same layout, same components, same tokens. The only structural difference is that V2 puts an ambient backdrop behind the shell — so the glass finally has something to be glass against.

Root cause

Glass over an opaque page is just grey

This is not a tuning problem. It is a compositing result, and it holds for every browse screen in the app.

bgBase#0A0B0F
+
surfaceGlassrgba(30,34,44,.55)
=
composited#14171E
A flat grey card.

No border, shadow, blur or shader can recover depth that was never in the buffer. The fix has to be behind the glass, not on it.

01

Nothing behind the glass

AppShell.kt:49 paints one opaque bgBase. Every browse screen — Home, Settings, Movies, Series, Favorites, Guide — is in the state above. That's most of the app.

02

Blur over video is impossible today

All three player surfaces use media3's default SurfaceView, which composites on its own layer and punches a hole through the window. Compose draws above it and can never sample those pixels — so the HUD is translucent but razor-sharp.

03

Glass nested in glass compounds to opaque

HUD buttons at 55% sit on a HUD bar at 72%. Alpha multiplies to roughly 87% effective opacity — which is why they render as solid black tiles floating on a translucent bar.

04

Two of seven optical ingredients

V1 shipped translucent fill and a lit edge. Missing: backdrop blur, vibrancy, specular rim, refraction, adaptive tint.

Worth saying out loud

The V1 companion mockup used backdrop-filter 24 times. The browser rendered real blur; the Kotlin never could. That gap between the pitch and the build is exactly what V2 closes — and it's why every mockup below is annotated with what actually makes it work on device.

Two fixes

The bar blurs because the player moves to a TextureView, putting video pixels inside the Compose draw pass. The buttons stop being black bricks because nested controls drop to tint-and-hairline instead of a second fill.

The one real risk in this plan

TextureView costs an extra GPU texture copy and renders on the UI thread's context. On a Fire TV Stick or a weak Amlogic box at 4K that is measurable. It ships behind a Settings toggle, default on only for API 31+ non-low-RAM devices, and it is the only phase that can regress playback.

Screen · Settings

The screen with no artwork to lean on

Settings has no posters, so it's the honest test of the ambient layer: the accent mesh has to carry the whole page on its own. Switch the mockup theme in the header — light is where glass is hardest, and it gets equal billing in this spec.

Light theme

In light mode borderGlass stays a dark ink edge — a white card with a white edge on a near-white page disappears. Vibrancy is also dialled back: saturation boost on an already bright backdrop pushes text contrast below AA fast.

Look at

The off switch and the segmented indicator. On the left the switch track is solid surface3 and the indicator is a flat accent pill — two opaque slugs with nothing behind them. On the right the backdrop reads straight through both: the track holds its shape on a hairline, and the indicator became a lens — brighter and clearer than the glass it slides in, not paint laid on top.

Tint, never a fill

A nested control gets white at ~11% plus the hairline. It must not get its own blur either — stacking blur inside blur is the same compounding mistake as stacking fills, just more expensive.

Tonal chips keep their hue

A chip carrying meaning stays tinted with its own hue at low alpha and a hue-400 hairline, so quality, catch-up and selected stay distinguishable at three metres.

The indicator is a lens

Selection is the one place Apple never paints. The marker is clearer than its track — accent at ~30%, a bright specular rim, a soft accent glow outward. It's also the single best place to spend the lens() refraction, because it moves: the backdrop bends as the pill slides between segments, which is the effect people actually recognise as liquid glass.

Two things stay solid

LIVE keeps a solid red fill and the provider logo well keeps its dark plate in both themes — both carry white content that has to survive any backdrop. Legibility outranks material consistency.

Component family · channel tiles

Every tile becomes its own backdrop

The ambient layer gives the page something behind its glass. A tile needs the same thing one level down — and the material is already loaded. Sample one dominant colour from the logo Coil has already decoded, and the tile has a backdrop that can never clash with its own contents.

Look at

Three tiles, three different washes — each sampled from its own channel. On the left every thumb is the same grey surface2 with a dark logoWell plate dropped in the middle. On the right the well turned translucent and the tile carries a hint of the channel's own identity, so a row of tiles finally reads as a row of different things.

The well keeps a floor

It becomes glass over a fixed dark scrim, not pure glass. Color.kt:112 already records why it's pinned dark in both themes — provider logos are overwhelmingly white-on-transparent PNGs, and a light well made them vanish. That constraint doesn't go away.

Sampled, so it can't clash

An arbitrary backdrop behind an arbitrary logo is a gamble. A backdrop derived from that same logo is not — which is exactly why the tile well can go translucent while the player HUD's well stays a solid plate over arbitrary video.

Two stops, not a blur

A gradient, never a blurred bitmap. A live grid holds 40+ tiles; forty blurred bitmaps mid-scroll is the whole jank budget, and at tile size a blurred logo is mud rather than colour. Being this cheap is also why it's the one thing here that ships on Tier C.

Degradation

Three tiers, and the floor is today

The app ships to minSdk 23. Nothing here can be allowed to break a cheap box, so the worst case is defined as exactly what ships now.

TierConditionWhat renders
AAPI 33+, not low-RAMAmbient backdrop, blur, vibrancy, specular rim — plus optional lens() refraction, flag-off until reviewed on a real TV
BAPI 31–32, or low-RAMAmbient backdrop, blur, vibrancy. No runtime shaders, no drift animation
CAPI ≤ 30Exactly today's look. Static ambient gradient only — the known-good fallback for the entire spec

Contrast is the gate

Any surface dropping below 4.5:1 over its real backdrop gets a scrim stop or a denser fill. Measured on device, both themes — not judged by eye.

Motion is optional

Reduced-motion stops the drift and the artwork cross-fade. The layers stay; only the animation halts.

Focus stays on top

Every focusable still composes TvFocusable. The accent ring draws above the glass and is never baked into it.

Open decision blocking Phase 1: should the ambient artwork follow the focused item only (cheaper, more responsive) or the row's dominant palette (calmer, less flicker during fast D-pad scroll)?
